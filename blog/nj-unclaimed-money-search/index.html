State treasuries across the country are sitting on tens of billions of dollars that belongs to regular people — by common estimates, roughly one in ten Americans is owed unclaimed money right now. It's rarely dramatic: a utility deposit from an apartment you moved out of, a payroll check from a job you quit, dividends on stock you forgot you owned, a deposit your old bank mailed to your previous address.

Why money goes unclaimed in the first place

It's almost always an address problem. You moved; the deposit refund went to the old apartment. The account sat dormant; the bank merged three times and the mail stopped. The policyholder died and the insurer couldn't find the beneficiary. Unclaimed property laws exist because companies can't just keep that money — after the dormancy period they must hand it to the state, which acts as permanent custodian until the rightful owner shows up.

New Jersey's Unclaimed Property Administration (part of the State Treasury) takes in everything from forgotten payroll checks to safe deposit box contents. And it does not expire, and there's no penalty for waiting — but the sooner you file, the sooner you're paid, and box contents are eventually auctioned (the proceeds still wait in your name, but Great-Grandma's coins don't).

Searching well beats searching once

  • Search every name you've had. Maiden name, full legal name (not nicknames — "Bob" won't find "Robert"), hyphenated versions, and common misspellings of your surname.
  • Search your deceased relatives. Estates are where the big claims hide — and a deceased parent's name needs to be searched in every state they ever lived in, not just New Jersey.
  • Businesses are owed money too. If you own an LLC or had a sole proprietorship, search the entity name — vendor refunds, uncashed checks, and deposits escheat just like personal property.
  • Don't pay a finder. Heir-finder letters often arrive after states publish unclaimed-property lists — they found your money for free and want a percentage. The letter is actually a free tip: take the name they mention and claim it yourself at unclaimedproperty.nj.gov.

What a claim actually requires in New Jersey

Claims through the NJ Unclaimed Property Administration can be started online at unclaimedproperty.nj.gov. Typical requirements: proof of identity (driver's license or passport), proof of your connection to the last known address or the reported owner, and your Social Security number for verification. Stock and dividend claims need more — an old statement, a transfer agent record, or a brokerage confirmation. Estate claims need the death certificate and either a Surrogate's short certificate (if you're the executor) or proof you were the named beneficiary.

Expect a few weeks to a few months for processing, depending on claim complexity. The state is paying you back — nobody is rushing you out the door, but nobody is chasing you either. The claim only exists when you file it.

Found money is real money — put it to work

A few hundred recovered dollars is not a windfall, but it's the cheapest cash you'll ever find — and it behaves like any other dollar.
