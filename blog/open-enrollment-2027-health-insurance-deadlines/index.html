Open Enrollment 2027: Every Health Insurance Deadline on One Calendar

October 19, 2026 · Particulate LLC · 9-minute read

Fall open enrollment is live right now — and it runs on three different clocks that most people blur into one. Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period started October 15 and slams shut December 7. The ACA Marketplace opens November 1 and closes January 15 — but December 15 quietly decides whether your January has coverage or a gap. And if you're in New Jersey, Get Covered NJ stays open through January 31, one of the longest windows in the country.

Miss the right clock and the penalties are wildly different: a Medicare Part D late sign-up can become a permanent penalty that compounds monthly for the rest of your life, while missing the ACA window just means a year uninsured unless a life event opens a Special Enrollment Period. This guide puts every 2026–27 deadline on one page, explains what December 15 actually costs you, and includes a calculator that tells you exactly when your coverage starts based on when you enroll.

The trap is that the clocks look interchangeable. "Open enrollment ends in December" is true for Medicare and false for the ACA. "You can still enroll in January" is true for the ACA and false for Medicare. Every year, people miss December 7 for a Part D change because a commercial reminded them "open enrollment runs through January" — about the wrong program.

What December 15 actually costs you

December 15 is not the HealthCare.gov deadline — January 15 is. December 15 is the date your coverage must be selected by if you want it to start January 1. Enroll December 16 through January 15 and your coverage starts February 1, which sounds harmless until you're paying January premiums twice (old plan and new plan) or, worse, carrying no coverage for a month you didn't plan on.

The real dollar cost of missing December 15 lands on people between plans: if you're leaving a job-based plan December 31 and thought Marketplace coverage would pick up seamlessly, a January 31 enrollment means you're uninsured for January — one MRI, one urgent-care stitch kit, one prescription month at retail cash prices. It's the single most expensive two-week delay in American health insurance.

Medicare: why December 7 is the hard wall

During the Annual Enrollment Period (October 15 – December 7) you can switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, change Medicare Advantage plans, or pick a new standalone Part D drug plan. Every change takes effect January 1, 2027 — but the plan must receive your request by December 7. After that:

  • Medicare Advantage members get one escape hatch: the Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period, January 1 – March 31, 2027, allows a single switch to a different MA plan or back to Original Medicare plus a Part D plan. One switch. Use it or lose it.
  • Original Medicare members get nothing in Q1. You cannot use the Jan–Mar window to join an MA plan for the first time.
  • Don't confuse AEP with your 65th birthday window: Initial Enrollment is a 7-month period around your 65th month, and missing Part D at 65 carries a late-enrollment penalty of 1% of the national base premium per month you delayed, permanently.

While you're reviewing: premiums, deductibles, and drug formularies all change January 1, and the income surcharges (IRMAA) were locked in by your 2024 MAGI — our Medicare IRMAA calculator shows whether this year's plan review should include a surcharge appeal.

The ACA side: cliff math makes this year's plan choice different

For 2027 coverage, the enhanced premium tax credits are gone and the 400% FPL subsidy cliff is back — one dollar over the line and your credit is zero for the whole year. That makes November the right month to model your expected 2027 MAGI against the cliff before picking a metal level, not next April when it's too late. Run the numbers in our ACA Subsidy Calculator 2027, and remember the reconciliation rules: advance credits are trued-up on Form 8962 against Form 1095-A, and if actual income crosses 400% FPL you repay everything.

Also new: carriers are exiting markets in 20+ states. If your insurer leaves, the exchange's algorithm picks your replacement plan — which may not be the cheapest or the one your doctors take. Auto-renewal is not a strategy this year. Log in during November and actively choose.

New Jersey: the extended window plus a state subsidy

Get Covered NJ runs through January 31, 2027 — enroll by December 31 for January 1 coverage, in January for February 1. New Jersey also layers its own state-funded subsidy on top of the federal credit, which reaches households the federal cliff cuts off. If a federal calculator shows you $0 because you're over 400% FPL, run your numbers on GetCoveredNJ before concluding you're unsubsidized. Below 138% FPL, NJ FamilyCare — not the Marketplace — is usually the right door.

Your five-step enrollment checklist

  1. Decide which clock you're on (Medicare, Marketplace, or both if you're 65 and still working — employer coverage changes the math).
  2. Run the calculator above with the date you realistically expect to enroll, not the date you hope to.
  3. Marketplace: model your 2027 MAGI against the 400% cliff before choosing a metal level, then compare benchmark Silver, net-of-subsidy.
  4. Medicare: pull your plan's "Annual Notice of Change" letter (arrives by September 30), check your doctors and every drug you take against the 2027 formulary, and file any switch before December 7.
  5. Calendar the payment: coverage isn't live until the first premium clears — January 1 coverage that isn't paid for is January 1 coverage you don't have.
