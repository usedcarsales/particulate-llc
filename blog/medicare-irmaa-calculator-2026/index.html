Medicare IRMAA Calculator 2026: What the Income Surcharges Really Cost

October 16, 2026 · Particulate LLC · 8-minute read

Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period opened October 15, 2026 and closes December 7 — and for roughly 8% of beneficiaries, the most expensive line in their healthcare budget isn't the plan they're comparing. It's IRMAA: the Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount that stacks surcharges on top of your Part B and Part D premiums based on a tax return you filed two years ago.

The 2026 standard Part B premium is $202.90/month. But if your 2024 modified adjusted gross income crossed $109,000 as a single filer ($218,000 joint), you're paying up to $487.00 more per month for Part B alone — plus up to $91.00 more for Part D. That's as much as $6,936 a year in surcharges, and it follows you regardless of which plan you pick during enrollment.

The bracket edges are cliffs, not slopes. Single filer with 2024 MAGI of $109,000: zero surcharge. At $109,001, Part B jumps $81.20/month and Part D jumps $14.50/month — $1,148.40 a year for one dollar of income. The calculator below shows exactly where you sit.

How IRMAA works: the two-year lookback

IRMAA is determined by your modified adjusted gross income from two years earlier. Your 2026 surcharges are set by your 2024 tax return — the one you filed in spring 2025. Social Security receives that number directly from the IRS, matches it against the bracket table CMS publishes each fall, and sends you a notice (initial determination) if a surcharge applies.

This two-year gap is why IRMAA surprises people. The Roth conversion you did in 2024, the business you sold, the large capital gains harvest — all of it shows up in your Medicare bill in 2026, even if your income has since collapsed. And the moves you make on this year's return control your 2028 surcharges.

The $1-over problem: bracket edges are cliffs

IRMAA brackets have no phase-in. Cross a threshold by a single dollar and the full surcharge applies for the entire year — all twelve months, both Part B and Part D. The first edge is the steepest in percentage terms:

  • $109,000 → $109,001 (single): $95.70/month more = $1,148.40/year
  • $137,000 → $137,001: $144.70/month more = $1,736.40/year
  • $171,000 → $171,001: $145.00/month more = $1,740.00/year
  • $205,000 → $205,001: $144.60/month more = $1,735.20/year
  • $499,999 → $500,000: $48.40/month more = $580.80/year

That's why MAGI management matters more than plan shopping for beneficiaries near a boundary. Deferring a year-end bonus, harvesting a capital loss, or pushing a discretionary Roth conversion below a bracket edge can save four figures — on top of whatever the conversion itself does to your taxes.

What you can change during open enrollment — and what you can't

Between October 15 and December 7, 2026, you can switch Medicare Advantage plans, drop Medicare Advantage for Original Medicare (or back), and change Part D drug plans. Changes take effect January 1, 2027. What you cannot do: escape IRMAA by switching plans. The surcharge rides on top of whatever Part B and Part D coverage you hold.

What you can change is the future surcharge. Your 2026 tax return (filed in 2027) sets your 2028 brackets. For households hovering near an edge, this year-end is the planning window:

  • Size any Roth conversion to land under a bracket edge — the interaction with your overall retirement plan matters more than the conversion alone (see our New Jersey retirement planning guide).
  • Once you're 70½ and taking required minimum distributions, direct charitable gifts (QCDs) reduce AGI in a way itemized deductions don't.
  • Time large capital gains across tax years instead of stacking them into one.
  • Max traditional (not Roth) 401(k)/IRA/SEP contributions for any year you're within reach of an edge — MAGI counts income after those deductions.

How to appeal: Form SSA-44

If a life-changing event has cut your income well below your two-year-old MAGI, you can ask Social Security to recompute using current income with Form SSA-44 (Medicare Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount – Life-Changing Event). Qualifying events include retirement or reduced work hours, loss of income-producing property, loss of pension, an employer settlement, or divorce, plus a broad "other" category for circumstances like a large income drop. Approved appeals can lower your surcharge retroactively to the month after the event.

You cannot appeal simply because the brackets feel unfair, or because a one-time gain inflated one year. The event must genuinely and durably reduce your income. If that's you, file it this fall — don't wait for the December 7 plan deadline to think about it, since the SSA-44 process runs on its own track at Social Security, separate from enrollment.

Why this matters for the pre-65 crowd too

The MAGI brackets that produce IRMAA at 65 are the same income levers that govern Marketplace subsidies before it. If you're 60–64 and buying your own coverage, the ACA subsidy calculator for 2027 shows the opposite cliff — the one where crossing 400% of the poverty line zeroes out premium tax credits. Planning both boundaries together is the difference between a smooth glide into Medicare and a five-figure surprise.
