Medicare Switch Planner 2027: Change Plans Before December 7?

October 22, 2026 · Particulate LLC · 8-minute read

Medicare's Open Enrollment Period is live right now: it runs October 15 through December 7, 2026, and any switch you request by the deadline takes effect January 1, 2027. This is the one window each year when anyone with Medicare can move between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, swap Advantage plans, or change Part D drug coverage. Yet every December, two predictable mistakes happen: people in Original Medicare drop a Medigap policy for a "$0 premium" Advantage plan without realizing the move can be a one-way door, and people in Advantage plans with the wrong network or the wrong drug formulary let the deadline slide because comparing plans is miserable.

The planner below prices the three paths against each other using the real 2027 numbers: CMS caps Medicare Advantage out-of-pocket limits at $9,850 in-network ($14,800 combined) with a median plan limit around $6,550, while Original Medicare has no out-of-pocket maximum at all — the 20% Part B coinsurance runs forever unless you own a Medigap supplement. Part D drug exposure is capped at $2,400 for 2027 no matter which way you go.

If you're in Medicare Advantage, you effectively get two bites at the apple: December 7 and one more one-shot switch in the first quarter. If you're in Original Medicare with a Medigap policy, you get one — and the trip back may not be guaranteed.

The Medigap one-way door

Here's the decision most people get wrong. Original Medicare's 20% coinsurance is uncapped — a single hospitalization can generate thousands in Part B provider bills, which is exactly what a Medigap policy exists to absorb. Medicare Advantage, by contrast, caps your exposure at the plan's out-of-pocket limit ($6,550 median, $9,850 maximum in-network for 2027).

So the temptation is always the same: drop the $185/month Medigap policy for the $0-premium Advantage plan and save $2,220 a year. For a healthy person, the math often genuinely works. The trap is the return trip: outside your 6-month Medigap open enrollment window and a handful of special situations, Medigap insurers in most states can medical underwrite your application — pull medical records, exclude pre-existing conditions, or deny you outright. A few states (including New York and Connecticut) require Medigap availability year-round, and federal trial rights protect Advantage members in their first 12 months, but for most people in most states, dropping Medigap for Advantage is a bet you won't get sick later. The right move if you're considering it: switch during this window, keep meticulous notes on any new diagnoses, and know your trial-right deadline before you burn it.

Three rules worth more than the calculator

  1. Price the worst case, not the average case. Advantage limits risk by capping it; Medigap limits risk by absorbing it; Original Medicare alone doesn't limit it at all. If a $9,850 year would wreck you and you have no Medigap, that's the answer regardless of premiums.
  2. Check the network before the price. The cheapest Advantage plan is worthless if your specialists aren't in it or the prior-authorization maze delays a procedure you already have scheduled. Make your top three providers the first filter, not the last.
  3. Re-run your drugs. Formularies reshuffle every year — a drug that was tier 2 can jump to tier 4 with a much higher copay. The $2,400 Part D cap protects you from catastrophe, but the path to the cap matters for a twelve-month budget.
