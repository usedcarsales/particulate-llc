Social Security COLA 2027: Your 3.6% Raise, Calculated to the Dollar

October 21, 2026 · 9 min read · By Particulate LLC

On October 14, 2026, the Social Security Administration announced a 3.6 percent cost-of-living adjustment for 2027 — the largest COLA in four years, up from 2.8% for 2026. Nearly 72 million Social Security beneficiaries see the increase beginning with January 2027 payments; about 7.3 million SSI recipients see it a day early, in the payment dated December 31, 2026.

SSA's announced average: the typical retired worker's check rises about $75 a month (roughly $2,095 to $2,170). Your exact raise is 3.6% of your own benefit, rounded by SSA's own convention — a $2,095 check becomes $2,171, because unrounded amounts of $889.90+ round up. But averages hide the two things that determine whether this raise actually reaches your bank account: the Medicare Part B premium that comes out of the check, and the tax torpedo that can claim up to 85% of the increase if your other income sits near a threshold.

All rounding follows SSA's own convention — the same math your notice in the mail will use.

What the 3.6% COLA Actually Does to Your Check

A COLA is applied to your unrounded benefit, then the result is rounded following SSA's rules: benefits under $889.90 round down to the nearest whole dollar; benefits of $890 or more round up. That's why two retirees with nearly identical checks can see slightly different raises. The percentages below are SSA's own announced averages — your actual benefit depends on your work record, not the average.

The Three Traps in Your 3.6% Raise

1. The Medicare premium comes out first

Most retirees have the standard Part B premium deducted directly from their Social Security check. The 2027 premium is announced by CMS in mid-to-late November — if it rises faster than your COLA dollars, your net check shrinks even while your gross benefit grows. The hold-harmless rule protects most existing beneficiaries (their net check can't fall in dollars because of a Part B increase), but it does not protect you if you're paying an IRMAA surcharge for higher income, if you're a new Medicare enrollee in 2027, or if you have premiums billed directly rather than withheld. If any of those describe you, model the premium in the calculator above — the net-change line is the one to watch.

2. The tax torpedo can eat the raise

The COLA isn't taxable income itself — but a bigger benefit can push more of it into taxable range. Up to 50% of your benefit becomes taxable when provisional income (adjusted gross income + non-taxable interest + half your Social Security) crosses $25,000 single / $32,000 joint, and up to 85% above $34,000 / $44,000. A 3.6% raise moves every beneficiary up that ladder slightly. If you were already comfortably above the thresholds, the tax torpedo only touches a slice of the raise (the rest was already at the 85% cap). If you're just below a threshold, the COLA itself can newly push you into it — that's the expensive case, and the calculator flags your exact position.

3. IRMAA: the raise that costs you twice

Higher-income beneficiaries pay IRMAA surcharges on Medicare Parts B and D based on your tax return from two years ago (your 2027 surcharge is set by your 2025 MAGI). The trap compounds: your 2025 income determines your 2027 premium, and your 2027 premium is deducted from your 2027 COLA-raised check. One large IRA withdrawal or capital gain can echo for years. If you're near a bracket boundary (they start at $106,000 single / $212,000 joint for 2026), the two-year lookback makes Q4 of this year the right time to manage it — see our Medicare IRMAA calculator for the full bracket math.

When You'll Actually See the Money

  • October 14, 2026 — SSA announces the 3.6% COLA (done).
  • Mid-to-late November 2026 — your personalized COLA notice appears in your my Social Security Message Center, up to three weeks before mail delivery. You must be enrolled in paperless notices by November 18 to see it online; otherwise it comes by mail in December.
  • December 31, 2026 — SSI recipients receive their first increased payment (January 1 is a holiday).
  • January 2027 — Social Security retirement, survivors, and disability checks reflect the 3.6% COLA. Your one-page notice shows the exact dates and dollar amounts.
  • Late November 2026 — CMS announces 2027 Medicare Part B premiums; enrollees can see theirs in the my Social Security Message Center.

If You're Still Working in 2027: The Earnings Test Moved

Claiming before full retirement age while working means SSA withholds $1 of benefits for every $2 you earn above $25,560 in 2027 (up from $24,480). In the year you reach FRA, it's $1 per $3 above $67,920, and only earnings in months before FRA count. The withheld benefits aren't lost forever — SSA recalculates and credit them back at FRA — but the monthly cash flow hit is real. The calculator applies your age to the right limit automatically.

What the COLA Doesn't Fix

The COLA indexes your benefit to CPI-W — the basket urban wage earners buy. Retiree spending skews toward healthcare and long-term care, which historically outpace CPI-W. A 3.6% raise on a check that was claimed at 62 at a 30% permanent reduction is still a reduced check. The COLA compounds what you claimed; it never revisits it. If you haven't filed yet, the claiming decision is worth roughly 40x more than any COLA — run it through our claiming-age calculator before you file.
