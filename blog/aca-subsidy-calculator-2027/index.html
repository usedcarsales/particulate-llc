ACA Subsidy Calculator 2027: Did You Fall Off the 400% Cliff?

October 15, 2026 · Particulate LLC · 9-minute read

Open enrollment for 2027 health coverage starts November 1, 2026 — and this year it arrives with the biggest change to Marketplace subsidies since 2021: the enhanced premium tax credits expired. The American Rescue Plan temporarily removed the ACA's infamous "subsidy cliff," capping premiums for every income level and letting high earners qualify for a credit. Those rules ended with 2025. For 2027 coverage, if your household income exceeds 400% of the federal poverty line, your premium tax credit is zero — no matter how expensive premiums get in your area.

At the same time, the IRS published its 2027 indexing adjustments in Rev. Proc. 2026-26: the share of income you're expected to contribute toward a benchmark Silver plan now runs from 2.15% (up from 2.10%) to 10.22% (up from 9.96%). Median proposed individual-market rate increases for 2027 are around 15%, and the maximum out-of-pocket limit rises to $12,000. Translation: full-price premiums up, and more of the premium on you.

How the premium tax credit formula works

Your monthly premium tax credit is:

Benchmark Silver premium − (applicable percentage × household income) ÷ 12

The "benchmark" is the second-lowest-cost Silver plan available to you in your rating area. The applicable percentage is set by the IRS table below — it's the slice of your income the law expects you to pay for that benchmark before the credit kicks in. If your expected contribution already exceeds the benchmark premium, your credit is $0 even below the cliff. If your income exceeds 400% of the poverty line, the applicable percentage column simply stops — the credit is $0 and you pay the full premium, which is why crossing the line by even a dollar is so expensive.

Every band rose for 2027. The floor went from 2.10% to 2.15%, and the ceiling crossed into double digits: at 300–400% of FPL you're now expected to spend 10.22% of household income on the benchmark premium before any credit. On a $90,000 income, that's an expected contribution of $9,198 a year — $234 more than under the 2026 table, before any premium increase.

What changed for 2027 (and what to do about it)

  • The cliff is back. Income over 400% FPL = $0 federal credit. If you're near the line, November is when you should be planning MAGI, not filing-season April.
  • The expected contribution rose at every band — from +0.05 points at the bottom to +0.26 points at the top. Your credit shrinks even if premiums didn't move (and they moved up: median proposed increases are around 15%, reaching 29% in some states).
  • Repayment caps are gone. The ARPA-era limits on repaying excess advance credits expired with the enhanced subsidies. If you take advance credits based on an income estimate, then your actual MAGI lands above 400% FPL, you repay the entire credit with your return via Form 8962. Below the cliff, repayment follows the statutory sliding scale.
  • Out-of-pocket maximums rose to $12,000 (from $10,600) for a single person, so plan metal levels and HSAs deserve a fresh look — a cheap Silver after subsidy can still leave you exposed.
  • Insurers are exiting in 20+ states. Don't rely on auto-renewal: if your carrier leaves, the exchange algorithm picks your replacement plan. Hundreds of thousands of enrollees will need to actively choose.

Engineering your MAGI before the cliff

The cliff turns ordinary tax planning into subsidy planning. Contributions that reduce MAGI — traditional 401(k)/IRA/SEP contributions, HSA contributions, and the self-employed health insurance deduction — now directly protect a credit worth up to five figures. One caution for the pre-Medicare retirement crowd: a Roth conversion that nudges MAGI over the cliff line forfeits the entire year's credit, so size conversions against the 400% boundary (see our New Jersey retirement planning guide and the Social Security claiming calculator for how the pieces interact).

The reconciliation trap: Form 1095-A and Form 8962

If you take advance credits (most enrollees do — they're applied directly to the monthly premium), you reconcile at tax time. Your Marketplace sends Form 1095-A; the credit gets recomputed on Form 8962 using your actual income. Estimate too low and you owe the difference — uncapped if actual income crossed 400% FPL. Estimate too high and you get the excess back as a refundable credit. Given the 15% premium hikes and the removed repayment caps, this is the year to update your income projection on the Marketplace the moment your pay changes — not when you file. New Jersey: the longest enrollment window and a state subsidy on top

Get Covered NJ runs its own exchange, and New Jersey announced that open enrollment for the 2027 plan year runs November 1, 2026 through January 31, 2027 — two weeks longer than the federal window, one of the longest in the country. The state also layers its own state-funded subsidy on top of the federal credit (historically reaching households above the federal cliff), so if the calculator above shows $0 because you're over 400% FPL, don't assume you're out of options — run your numbers on GetCoveredNJ's own tool. And below 138% FPL, New Jersey's Medicaid expansion means NJ FamilyCare, not Marketplace subsidies, is usually the right door — which is why the dollar table above includes the 138% line.
