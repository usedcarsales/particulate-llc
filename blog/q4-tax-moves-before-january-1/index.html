Q4 Tax Moves to Make Before January 1

October 5, 2026 · 8 min read · By Particulate LLC

Every December, two kinds of people call a tax professional: the ones who made three smart moves in October and just need confirmation, and the ones who realize on December 28th that they owe a five-figure surprise. The difference between them is almost never income — it's timing.

The fourth quarter is the only part of the tax year you can still change. Here are the five moves that actually move the needle, with real numbers.

The clock that matters most: if you filed an extension, your 2025 federal return is due October 15, 2026 — 10 days from now. If that's you, finish the return first, then come back to this list. Estimated tax payment #4 for 2026 isn't due until January 15, 2027, but the rest of these moves expire December 31.

Move 1: Run the Safe Harbor Math Before You Send Q4 Estimated Taxes

The single most expensive Q4 mistake is guessing at your quarterly estimated payment. The IRS doesn't actually require you to be right about your income — it requires you to hit one of two "safe harbor" thresholds:

  • Prior-year safe harbor: pay 100% of your 2025 total tax (110% if your 2025 adjusted gross income was over $150,000), or
  • Current-year safe harbor: pay 90% of what you'll actually owe for 2026.

Hit either one and underpayment penalties are off the table — no matter how much you made.

Which one you pick depends entirely on whether 2026 was better or worse than 2025:

Your situationCheap shieldWhy
Income up sharply in 2026Prior-year (100%/110% of 2025 tax)You lock in last year's smaller number as your floor
Income down in 2026Current-year (90% of 2026 tax)90% of a smaller number beats 100% of a bigger one
Roughly flat incomeEither — take the bigger oneBoth shields work; keep cash

Worked example: freelancer, income up 40%

Say you owed $24,000 total tax in 2025 (AGI under $150K), and 2026 has been good — you're on track for $34,000 of tax. W-2 withholding isn't in the picture; this is all estimates.

ShieldMathRequired payment
Prior-year safe harbor100% × $24,000$24,000
Current-year safe harbor90% × $34,000$30,600

Prior-year shield wins by $6,600. You pay $24,000 in estimates across the year, are fully shielded from underpayment penalties, and settle the remaining ~$10,000 with your return in April 2027 — penalty-free. That's the whole trick: the safe harbor is a floor for penalties, not a ceiling on your bill.

Already sent $18,000 in estimates through September? Your Q4 payment due January 15, 2027 is just $6,000 — the remainder of the $24,000 shield, not some guessed "quarterly share."

Move 2: Max Retirement Contributions While There's Still Payroll

Retirement contributions are the only Q4 move that both cuts your tax bill and builds net worth. The deadlines split cleanly:

  • December 31, 2026: 401(k) employee deferrals ($24,500 limit, plus $8,000 catch-up if 50+) — these come out of payroll, so the window closes with your last paycheck. Solo 401(k) and defined-benefit plan setup paperwork also generally must be done by year-end.
  • April 15, 2027: SEP-IRA and traditional IRA contributions ($17,000 IRA limit for 2026, deductible only if you're under the income phase-outs). These can wait — but only if you have the cash later.

The catch: the employer portion of your solo 401(k) contribution can be made up until the filing deadline, but the plan must exist by December 31. If you had a strong self-employed year and no plan exists yet, October is when you open one — not December.

Quick math on the deferral: every $10,000 you defer into a traditional 401(k) at a 24% federal marginal rate saves $2,400 in federal tax alone (more with NJ state tax, which also allows the deferral deduction). Deferring the full $24,500 saves roughly $5,900–$7,300 federal depending on bracket.

Move 3: Bunch Deductions or Spread Them — Decide Now, Not in April

Since the standard deduction jumped past $15,000 (single) and $31,200 (married filing jointly) for 2026, itemizing only pays in years when you deliberately stack deductible expenses. Q4 is when you decide:

  • Bunching: pull next year's charitable gifts, a planned medical procedure, or prepayable property taxes into this December. One fat itemized year, then take the standard deduction next year.
  • Deferring: if you already itemized big this year (or bunched last year), push discretionary deductions into January instead — they seed next year's itemized stack.

Two traps: state prepayment rules differ (NJ property tax prepayment only works if the municipality actually receives it by December 31), and bunching only beats the standard deduction when the itemized total actually clears it. Run both years' numbers side by side before writing any December checks.

Move 4: Business Owners — Q4 Is Your Entity Health Check

If you run an LLC or S-corp (hello, fellow small business), three things belong on the calendar before year-end:

  1. Reasonable compensation check (S-corps): if you took distributions with too-low salary, fix the final quarter's payroll now. The IRS doesn't accept retroactive raises in April.
  2. Equipment & Section 179/bonus depreciation: assets must be placed in service by December 31 — not ordered, not paid for, in service. A machine delivered January 3rd is a 2027 deduction.
  3. QBI look-ahead: qualified business income deduction planning for pass-throughs interacts with retirement contributions and estimated payments — a good reason to do moves 1, 2, and 4 in one sitting, not piecemeal.

Also: if your LLC has been funding itself with informal owner loans instead of capital, clean the ledger before year-end. Commingled books are the #1 thing that pierces the liability shield you formed the LLC for in the first place.

Move 5: Harvest Losses (and Check Your Winners)

Tax-loss harvesting got harder — wash-sale rules now sweep through "substantially identical" assets, and the IRS tightened coverage of related-party and foreign-broker transactions. But the core move still works: sell positions sitting below basis before December 31 to realize losses that offset realized gains (and up to $3,000 of ordinary income, with carryforward).

The Q4 check most people skip: look at your winners too. If you're in a low bracket this year — or have losses already banked — it can be the cheapest moment of the whole cycle to realize long-term gains. Same rate visibility, opposite trade.

Crypto positions count: digital assets follow the same capital gain/loss mechanics post the 2025 reporting reforms, and broker reporting means the IRS sees what you see. Harvest with the same discipline you'd apply to equities.
